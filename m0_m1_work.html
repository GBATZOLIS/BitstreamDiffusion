One model, two kinds of protein information

A protein has a sequence—its amino-acid letters—and a 3D backbone—the path those residues take through space. Bitstream Diffusion asks whether both can share one binary interface, so the same model can generate either one, predict one from the other, or fill in missing parts.

01
1

Why

Protein design often needs separate models for sequence and structure. A shared interface could combine tasks.

2

What

Encode each residue as 5 sequence bits plus 13 structure bits: one 18-bit patch.

3

How

Hide or add noise to the track to generate; hold the other track fixed as context.

4

Outcome

M0 learned the relationship, especially 3D → sequence. Absolute protein quality is still weak.

The key distinction

“The model uses its input” and “the model produces a useful protein” are different claims. M0 supports the first. M1 must be designed to test the second.

Sequence
The amino-acid letters that define a protein.
Backbone
The 3D path of the protein chain, without detailed side chains.
Conditioning
Information held fixed while the model fills what is missing.
Tokenizer
A codec that compresses biology into symbols or bits.
TM-score
A 0–1 measure of global shape similarity; higher is better.
OOD
Proteins unlike those seen during training.

The 30-second answer

M0 and M1 use the same core representation and task interface. They differ mainly in scientific purpose, corpus scale, model capacity, compute, and the strength of claim they are intended to support.

Session 1 · M0 against its baselines

First ask whether the mechanism adds signal

The primary question is not whether a 37.7M pilot outranks a 650M foundation model. It is whether the bitstream model uses protein sequence and structure information at all, and whether that benefit survives a controlled ablation.

01

What M0 establishes—and what it does not

Established: correct sequence or backbone context changes the requested output more than a shuffled version of that context or no context. This is evidence that the joint 18-bit model learned a cross-modal relationship.

Not established: bit encoding is better than categorical tokens, or that the outputs fold. Those require a matched representation ablation and independent refolding.

Protocol and claim boundary

Deterministic length-stratified subset of 32 targets from 128 CAMEO-labelled cached test rows (length 60–383), four conditional samples per target, seeds 20260719–20260722, and 250 Heun steps. Confidence intervals on the recovery and LFQ-bit-accuracy lifts are paired bootstrap 95% intervals over targets; TM differences are reported as differences of means (not bootstrapped). This is one checkpoint evaluated with four independent sampler seeds; interactive examples use seed 20260719.

The cache contains amino-acid and LFQ structure-token references, not native coordinates. Therefore structure metrics compare decoded predictions with decoded LFQ references; they measure model-plus-tokenizer behavior and cannot be presented as native-backbone accuracy. ProteinMPNN, ESMFold/self-consistency, Foldseek/native-structure novelty, multimer/ligand tests, and wet-lab validation remain outstanding.

There is one Transformer position per residue. Sequence and structure are not text tokens or coordinate vectors inside the diffusion model: each is converted to a fixed-width binary channel, concatenated, corrupted, and denoised.

Sequence tokenizer

Each of the 20 canonical amino acids maps directly to a 5-bit code. There is no BPE, wordpiece, or k-mer vocabulary. Because 5 bits permit 32 values, 12 codewords are invalid and must be excluded or mapped by constrained decoding; raw invalid-code rate must still be reported.

3D structure tokenizer

A frozen DPLM-2 lookup-free quantizer maps each residue’s N/Cα/C/O geometry to 1 of 8,192 states: exactly 13 bits, most-significant bit first. Every 13-bit pattern is valid. The frozen decoder reconstructs a backbone; its reconstruction error is a ceiling on the generative model.

How one model performs many tasksTraining samples a task mask. For sequence→3D, sequence bits are observed and structure bits are noised. For 3D→sequence, the reverse. For joint generation both are noised; for infilling only selected residue patches are noised. Separate noise levels σseq and σstruct tell the denoiser which channel is uncertain.

Tokenizer decision · DPLM-2 versus Yeti

Yeti is an LFQ tokenizer—not an alternative to LFQ

Both systems quantize each residue into one of 8,192 states, equivalent to 13 binary dimensions. The choice is between two learned meanings for those bits and two coordinate decoders; their token IDs are not interchangeable.

Current M0 · DPLM-2 LFQ

  • Frozen pretrained encoder and IPA-style decoder.
  • Already used to build all 220,475 M0 pairs and the trained checkpoint.
  • Keeps the structure codec external, making the Bitstream denoiser easier to isolate scientifically.
  • Current weakness: M0 has not measured the tokenizer reconstruction ceiling on native coordinates, and its generated token distribution shifts from test data.

Yeti · LFQ + flow decoder

  • 62.5M parameters, 8,192-state LFQ, trained end-to-end for reconstruction and generative usefulness.
  • Reports TM-score 0.95–0.97 reconstruction and 98–99% intra-structure token diversity across its tests.
  • Its tokens support a published 224M scratch-trained joint co-generator with scTM 0.70 and pLDDT 76.12.
  • As of the v1 preprint, the authors say source code will be released; a reproducible drop-in checkpoint is not yet established here.
RecommendationKeep DPLM-2 LFQ for the completed M0 result. For M1, treat Yeti as the leading tokenizer ablation once code and weights are available: retokenize the same frozen corpus, train the same Bitstream architecture and budget, then compare tokenizer ceiling, bit entropy, joint scTM/pLDDT, novelty and throughput. Do not swap Yeti tokens into m0_v2_b at inference—the bit semantics differ, so the model must be retrained.

Where it sits in protein design

Bitstream Diffusion is a candidate generator. It covers early sequence/backbone proposal and some geometric conditioning. It does not replace physical refinement, docking, functional assays, or experimental validation.

1 · intent
Specify

length, known sequence/backbone, motif geometry

2 · this model
Generate

sequence, backbone, or both

3 · triage
Cross-check

refolding, designability, novelty, geometry

4 · downstream
Refine

side chains, energy, docking, MD

5 · evidence
Test

expression, folding, binding, activity

De novo protein design

candidate generation

Generate a backbone, a sequence, or a coupled sequence–backbone pair. Requires independent refolding and experimental validation.

Motif scaffolding

geometric stage

Keep an active-site or structural motif fixed and generate surrounding protein. Does not model catalytic chemistry or binding energetics.

Sequence redesign

supported surface

Given a backbone, sample compatible sequences; useful before side-chain packing and energy-based ranking.

Structure prediction

research task

Sequence→backbone is supported, but M0/M1 are not positioned as replacements for AlphaFold/ESMFold without benchmark evidence.

Binder design

not target-aware

No target chain or interface representation in the stated monomer protocol. An interface-aware multimer extension is required.

Ligand design or docking

not represented

No small-molecule graph, ligand coordinates, protein pocket context, pose, or affinity objective. Use dedicated tools downstream.

Can M0 make the Yeti unconditional co-generation claim?

No—not from the present evaluation. Yeti evaluates 500 jointly generated sequence–structure pairs with ESMFold pLDDT, scTM/scRMSD, CATH structural novelty and ProteinMPNN recovery. M0 evaluated separate sequence and backbone marginals; it has no paired joint self-consistency result.

Strongest supported M0 wording: “A 37.7M-parameter Bitstream Diffusion model trained on 220,475 paired examples learns measurable bidirectional conditioning between amino-acid and 3D structure-token channels.” That is a compact multimodal-learning claim, not a plausible-protein co-generation claim.

What full completion actually requires

The sections below preserve the methodological detail needed for a computer-science or computational-biology review. They are collapsed by default so the main story remains readable.

Decontamination: preventing train–test leakage

Decontamination is more than removing identical accessions. Protein families contain homologs with different names, and predicted structures may be derived from sequences that overlap an evaluation set. Leakage can make folding, inverse-folding, novelty, and designability results look better without representing generalization.

M0 must still do

  1. Freeze evaluation accessions and immutable files before rebuilding training data.
  2. Remove exact sequence and coordinate duplicates across AFDB, PDB and CAMEO sources.
  3. Search every training candidate against every evaluation sequence with MMseqs2; exclude matches above 30% identity with at least 80% bidirectional coverage, recording exact parameters.
  4. Audit nearest structural neighbours with Foldseek and report similarity strata.
  5. Relabel current results “CAMEO-labelled test,” not “temporal test,” unless release dates and cutoff are reconstructed.

M1 must do

  1. Attach sequences and validate one-to-one residue/coordinate alignment before tokenization.
  2. Apply exact hashes, quality filters and evaluation exclusions to the full union.
  3. Cluster all surviving candidates at 30% identity, then assign whole clusters to train/val/test.
  4. Keep external time-split, topology, motif and private-recent-PDB sets entirely outside the internal split.
  5. Publish a manifest with source versions, cutoff dates, hashes, cluster IDs, exclusion reasons and counts at every stage.

Recommended operational rule: freeze tests → build candidate union → exact deduplication → evaluation homology exclusion → quality filtering → clustering → split assignment. Never split first and decontaminate later.

Evaluation sets and metrics by task

External evaluations

  • CAMEO 2022 or a verifiable recent-PDB time split for sequence→structure.
  • CATH 4.3 inverse-folding splits for structure→sequence.
  • CATH S20/topology splits for tokenizer and structural OOD analysis.
  • CASP14/15 only when training cutoffs make them genuinely held out.
  • A versioned motif-scaffolding set plus a private recent-PDB audit.

Common reporting rules

  • At least three seeds, paired examples, identical lengths, sample counts, noise schedule and function evaluations.
  • Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals; include failed decodes in denominators.
  • Report mean, median, distribution and per-length/per-topology strata—not only cherry-picked structures.
  • Separate model quality from tokenizer reconstruction ceiling.

Use 200k optimizer steps initially: at global batch 512 this matches the example exposure of a former 400k × 256 plan. For mixed lengths, the authoritative training budget should be valid residue patches, not padded examples.

Scientific controls for scale. Train 37.7M on both M0 and M1 data to isolate the data effect; train 125M and ≈393M on M1 to estimate the capacity effect. Without these factorial controls, describe M0→M1 only as a system-scale comparison.
Completion gates: when may each protocol be called complete?

M0 complete when

  • Frozen dataset and checkpoint manifests reproduce counts and hashes.
  • Tokenizer round-trip and geometry ceiling are measured.
  • Folding and inverse folding beat shuffled and unconditional controls with confidence intervals.
  • Joint samples do not collapse in either modality.
  • Sequence-only retention is acceptable against the warm-start model.
  • Current CAMEO claim is corrected or a defensible time split is rebuilt.
  • All supported task paths have smoke tests; only benchmarked tasks are claimed as validated.

M1 complete when

  • The paired corpus exists with verified sequences, structures, provenance and final counts.
  • Decontamination and cluster-first splits are frozen before training.
  • Two-node EOS launch, resume, determinism, throughput and failure recovery are tested.
  • Activation checkpointing and memory controls are implemented and verified.
  • External temporal, topology, inverse-folding and motif evaluations are complete.
  • Data/capacity ablations separate sources of improvement.
  • Model card, data card, checkpoints, configs and evaluation artifacts permit independent reproduction.

End-to-end workflow and M0 architecture

Biology → bits → denoiser → judged design

The same 18-bit residue patch serves every direction. The task is selected by marking each modality observed, noisy, or absent; observed values are clamped throughout reverse diffusion.

🧬

1 · Inputs

Amino acids plus optional backbone atoms N, Cα, C, O; length and modality masks define the request.

▦

2 · Frozen codecs

5 sequence bits encode 20 residues. DPLM LFQ maps each residue backbone to 13 structure bits.

18

3 · Joint patch

Concatenate 5 + 13 channels per residue. State masks distinguish observed, target/noisy, absent and padding.

σ

4 · EDM noise

Independent sequence and structure noise levels train marginals, conditionals, joint generation and infilling.

◎

5 · Bitstream core

37.7M parameters · width 384 · 13 Transformer blocks · 8 heads · RoPE + AdaLN · self-conditioning.

↶

6 · Reverse solve

Heun denoising; 250 steps in this screen, 250 configured for full runs. Re-clamp observed tracks after every step.

✓

7 · Decode + judge

20-code MAP sequence and frozen LFQ coordinate decoder; compare correct, shuffled and absent controls with task metrics.

Training path

Warm-start from the sequence checkpoint → freeze the trunk for 2k steps → continue with 0.1× trunk learning rate → mix 40% sequence replay → maintain EMA and periodic checkpoints. Independent modality masks teach all four directions without separate heads.

Scientific evaluation path

Freeze checkpoint and targets → sample matched lengths/budgets → compare correct conditioning with shuffled and unconditional controls → decode → score token accuracy, TM-score, lDDT, RMSD, recovery, sequence plausibility, clashes and diversity → bootstrap paired targets → inspect distributions and overlays.

Decision logicA useful protein-design system must pass both axes: conditioning sensitivity (the requested input changes the right output) and absolute biological quality (foldability, geometry, novelty, diversity and task success). M0_v2_b passes the first mechanistic test but not the complete second axis.
